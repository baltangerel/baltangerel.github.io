AER304 Aerospace Design III · University of Sheffield · 2023–24

Brick Flyer: VTOL Quadplane UAS

I was Project Manager of a six-person team that designed, built, certified and flew a drone that takes off like a quadcopter, flies like a plane and lands itself. It was built for a simulated industry tender for a search-and-location aircraft.

My role

  • Led the team of six discipline leads (structures, flight dynamics, avionics, ground systems, integration), and owned the project plan and milestones.
  • Reported to the client every fortnight with "5-box" status updates covering progress, risks, cost and issues.
  • Owned the budget and the final financial review, and was responsible for all formal documents, including the safety case that earned our Flight Test Certificate.
  • Turned around a poor start: after weak early documents, I introduced weekly meetings with a set purpose and a rebuilt plan. From then on we stayed on schedule.

The aircraft

1.4 m wingspan · 2.1 kg · 4 VTOL motors + 1 puller motor · CFRP frame, foamboard wings · ArduPilot autopilot

Key decisions

  • Test rig before airframe: the "iron bird" let us sort out the avionics early, and it caught a propeller clash that would have destroyed the aircraft in flight.
  • Analysis-led structure: FEA showed CFRP gave the lowest displacement for its weight, so we used it for the frame. The wing peaked at 1.42 MPa under a 3× overload, against foamboard's 28 MPa strength.
  • Test early, fix early: reaching the drone nets two weeks early exposed underpowered props in time to upgrade from 5″ to 6″.
  • Designed for speed: quick-release spar-and-dowel wings got us flight-ready in about 8 minutes, against a 10-minute requirement.

Flight day: crash, diagnosis, recovery

The first forward flight crashed after a mode change. By lining up the onboard video with the flight logs, we found the cause: Loiter was selected instead of RTL, and the automatic bank pushed the airspeed past the VTOL-assist limit, so the lift motors cut out and the aircraft stalled. We repaired it on site and flew again the same day, and it was our best flight of the project.

We met the requirements for propulsion, flight modes, transport box, set-up time and safety. We missed two: mass was 2.1 kg against a 2 kg limit, and flight time was about 5 minutes against a 10-minute target. Both are the first targets for a next iteration.

What I'd take into my next project

  • Set up structure from day one: regular meetings with a clear purpose, and a plan the whole team believes in.
  • Build time for prototypes and testing into the plan. Problems found early are cheap to fix.
  • Check the small things and label the switches. A wrong parameter delayed certification, and an unlabelled switch caused our crash.
